# Appendix A. Risk register

*Last reviewed: September 27, 2026*

This register lists the risks that could stop the colony from reaching its crew gate or harm the first crew. It covers the plan from the optional 2033 pathfinder to the end of the first crew mission in 2046, plus two expansion-era risks from [chapter 15](15.%20Terraforming%20and%20the%20long%20view.md) that could harm the colony or its standing ([R37](#r37), [R38](#r38)). Expansion questions that threaten neither a crew nor a gate, such as how fast aerogel degrades, stay in chapter 15 as research questions. Numbers follow [chapter 2](02.%20Baseline%20and%20budgets.md). Where an entry rests on engineering judgment rather than a source, the evidence column says so.

A reader who follows an ID from a chapter, such as [R19](#r19), lands on that row of the register, and its Chapter column lists every chapter that links the row, so the way back is one click. A reader who opens the appendix cold should start with "Priorities", which ranks all 40 risks by score and links each to its row, and use "How to read the ratings" to decode the letters and numbers in the wide table.

Use the score to decide where to look first. Because it multiplies likelihood, impact, and detectability, a risk that stays hidden until it strikes outranks a visible one of the same size: integration debt ([R22](#r22)) ranks first, while crew dose ([R19](#r19)), certain but known years ahead, scores only 12. Use the impact rating to decide what never drops out of view: the highest-impact risks are reviewed at every gate whatever their score. [Chapter 14](14.%20Critical%20challenges.md) groups the risks into seven areas and orders them by the highest score in each; this appendix scores every risk, names what to watch, and assigns an owner.

## How to read the ratings

Likelihood, in the register's L column, is the chance that the risk occurs within the plan horizon, given the evidence in September 2026. Its values are L for low (below 10%), M for medium (10–50%), and H for high (above 50%), so an L in the L column means low likelihood.

Impact (I), on a scale of 1–5:

1. Local inconvenience; no schedule effect.
2. Degraded operations for days to weeks.
3. Loss of a capability or a delay of months.
4. Slip of a launch window, loss of a wave's major element, or a lasting health effect on the crew.
5. Loss of crew, loss of the colony, or end of the program.

Detectability (D), on a scale of 1–5, where 5 is hardest to detect:

1. Visible years ahead or monitored continuously with a long lead time.
2. Monitored, with weeks of warning.
3. Seen only through indirect indicators.
4. Detectable only at onset.
5. Latent until it causes a failure, such as an interface error.

The priority score is L × I × D, with L counted as 1, 2, or 3. It ranges from 1 to 75 and ranks where attention should go. Any risk with impact 5, or with likelihood H and impact 4, is reviewed at every gate whatever its score.

Residual risk is the owner's judgment of what remains after the preventive controls, stated as Low, Medium, or High.

## How owners review the register

> Assumption: The review process below is this book's proposal, modeled on standard program risk practice.

Each risk has one owner, named by role. The owner keeps the entry current, watches its early warnings, and reports at each gate review, held about three months before each window, and at a mid-cycle review between windows. Once Wave 1 lands, the Orchestrator tracks the early warnings continuously and flags any indicator that crosses a threshold to the owner and the Orchestrator safety board, without waiting for a scheduled review.

The Orchestrator safety board owns risks that cross systems ([R12](#r12), [R22](#r22), [R29](#r29)) and settles disputes between owners. A risk is closed only with evidence, such as a flown demonstration or a year of on-site data, and closed risks stay in the register with their closing evidence. New risks get the next free ID, and IDs are never reused, so links from chapters stay valid.
