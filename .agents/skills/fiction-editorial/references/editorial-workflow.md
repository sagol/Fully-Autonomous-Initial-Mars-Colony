# Editorial Workflow Reference

Edit from large-scale causality toward small-scale correctness. Each pass has a defined change budget so that a copyeditor does not accidentally rewrite character logic and a developmental editor does not spend time polishing scenes that should be cut.

## Pass 0 — Freeze the working truth

Before editing, identify:

- accepted manuscript version
- current outline / book architecture
- current canon and timeline
- author decisions that are not open for reconsideration
- facts and numerical constraints
- POV and voice rules
- target audience / genre promise
- scope of this edit

If a requested change conflicts with a frozen decision, flag it instead of silently overriding it.

## Pass 1 — Developmental structure

Allowed changes: large.

Ask:

- What is the book's central dramatic question?
- Does the protagonist cause the story through choices?
- Is the opposition strong enough to force adaptation?
- Does each major turn narrow or transform available options?
- Are stakes personal as well as external?
- Does the midpoint or equivalent change the meaning of the problem, not merely increase volume?
- Does the climax force a costly choice that resolves the book's thematic contradiction?
- Does the ending close the local arc while changing the series state irreversibly?
- Are subplots exerting pressure on the main story or merely coexisting with it?
- Are reveals set up so they reorganize earlier material on reread?
- Is there a quieter, stronger version of any spectacle that would create a harder choice?

Typical actions:

- reorder or remove scenes
- merge redundant characters or functions
- strengthen motives
- move revelations
- plant missing causes before effects
- replace coincidence with consequence
- reduce exposition that delays the dramatic engine

Do not line-edit prose here unless a local example is needed to demonstrate the problem.

## Pass 2 — Chapter and scene function

Allowed changes: medium to large inside the chapter.

For every scene, identify:

- POV
- immediate want
- opposition
- escalation
- information change
- decision
- consequence
- exit state

A scene without a state change may still work as deliberate atmosphere, transition, compression, or recovery, but it should be conscious and proportionate.

Check scene entrances and exits. Enter as late as comprehension permits and leave after the consequential beat, not after everyone explains what it meant.

Check chapter rhythm:

- action / reaction balance
- compression / expansion
- scene / summary balance
- tension release
- spatial variety
- POV distribution
- ending pressure

Avoid forcing a cliffhanger onto every chapter. A commitment, emotional rupture, new objective, discovery, or altered interpretation can create stronger forward motion.

## Pass 3 — Continuity, logic, and factual integrity

Allowed changes: minimal unless a contradiction requires escalation to the author.

Audit:

- dates and elapsed time
- travel time and geography
- ages
- injuries and recovery
- possessions
- resource consumption
- technical capacities
- who knows what when
- promises and deadlines
- institutional authority
- names, ranks, titles, terminology
- previously established limits
- cause/effect order

For hard SF, classify every challenged item as:

- external fact
- project design choice
- stated assumption
- intentional speculation
- error

Do not "fix" uncertainty by inventing certainty.

For numerical systems, trace quantities back to the canonical budget or source. A beautiful paragraph cannot spend the same oxygen, propellant, power, time, or spare part twice.

### Contradiction report

Record:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Claim A | Earlier or higher-authority statement |
| Claim B | Conflicting statement |
| Authority | Which source outranks the other |
| In-world reconciliation | Possible / impossible |
| Proposed repair | Smallest coherent change |
| Downstream impact | Scenes, clues, timeline, resources affected |

## Pass 4 — Character, arc, and POV

Allowed changes: medium.

For every important character ask:

- What do they want in this scene?
- What are they afraid of losing?
- What are they pretending not to want?
- What do they know?
- What do they misunderstand?
- What tactic are they using?
- Does their choice fit the current state of their arc?
- Is any action present only because the plot needs it?
- Does pressure reveal a contradiction or merely make them louder?

POV checks:

- no unearned access to another mind
- no knowledge the viewpoint lacks
- perceptions match expertise and bias
- internal interpretation is distinguishable from objective fact
- narrative distance remains deliberate
- multi-POV transitions are explicit enough to avoid accidental head-hopping

A character should not "learn" the same lesson repeatedly unless relapse itself is the point.

## Pass 5 — Dialogue and subtext

Allowed changes: medium at sentence level, small at plot level.

Every line should do at least one of these:

- pursue an objective
- resist another objective
- conceal
- test
- bargain
- threaten
- reassure
- provoke
- deflect
- reveal accidentally
- change status
- make a choice

Delete dialogue that exists only so two informed characters can tell each other facts they already know.

Check differentiation through:

- sentence length
- vocabulary
- precision
- humor
- directness
- interruption
- tolerance for silence
- preferred metaphors
- technical jargon
- emotional avoidance

Subtext is not vagueness. The characters know what they are doing even when they do not state it.

## Pass 6 — Line, image, and rhythm

Allowed changes: sentence and paragraph level.

Improve:

- specificity
- verb strength
- sensory selectivity
- image freshness
- cadence
- paragraph shape
- transitions
- redundant explanation
- abstract emotion replaced by perceivable consequence when appropriate

Preserve deliberate:

- fragments
- repetition
- dialect
- awkwardness
- long sentences
- sparse prose
- punctuation habits
- technical density

A uniform "clean" style can erase character and tension.

## Pass 7 — Anti-generic / anti-AI-pattern pass

Allowed changes: sentence and paragraph level. This is a prose-quality pass, not an authorship detector.

Watch for recurrent model-like defaults:

- staging phrases before the real point
- one-line dramatic closers that only repeat the preceding paragraph
- aphorisms that sound profound but add no specific meaning
- fake objections nobody raised
- forced groups of three
- repeated sentence templates
- excessive em dashes used as universal connectors
- stacked qualifiers
- generic intensifiers
- abstract claims where concrete evidence already exists
- explanation after an image that the reader has already understood
- every paragraph ending with the same kind of "meaning" sentence
- all characters speaking with identical polished fluency
- over-signaled contrasts ("not X but Y") when the negative half is unnecessary
- manufactured "cinematic" fragments
- identical cliffhanger cadence chapter after chapter

Do not remove a pattern merely because it appears once. Preserve patterns that clearly belong to the author's voice, genre, or character.

## Pass 8 — Copyedit and proof

Allowed changes: mechanical only unless explicitly authorized.

Check:

- grammar
- punctuation
- spelling
- capitalization
- quotation style
- typography
- names
- repeated/missing words
- formatting
- chapter numbering
- scene-break consistency

Do not change motive, facts, chronology, or voice in a proof pass.

## Editorial change budgets

| Pass | Structure | Facts | Voice | Sentence rewriting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Developmental | High | Flag | Preserve intent | Low |
| Scene | Medium | Preserve | Preserve | Medium |
| Continuity | Low | Correct/flag | Preserve | Low |
| Character/POV | Medium | Preserve | High sensitivity | Medium |
| Dialogue | Low | Preserve | High sensitivity | Medium |
| Line | None | Preserve | High sensitivity | High |
| Anti-generic | None | Preserve | High sensitivity | Medium |
| Proof | None | Mechanical only | Preserve | Minimal |

## Editorial issue format

For meaningful issues, use:

- **Issue**
- **Evidence**
- **Why it matters**
- **Priority**: critical / high / medium / optional
- **Smallest useful fix**
- **Downstream effects**

Do not bury the three most consequential problems under dozens of micro-notes.

## Quality rubric

A useful review can score or discuss these dimensions separately:

- constraint / brief integration
- causality and coherence
- character agency
- originality
- voice and diction
- concreteness
- sentence rhythm and syntax
- POV control and narrative distance
- structure and pacing
- tone
- imagery and sensory strategy
- dialogue amount and subtext
- formal experimentation
- closure / ending cadence
- continuity
- thematic pressure
- genre promise

Do not collapse all of these into one opaque number.

## Revision modes

### Diagnose only
Report problems and options; make no prose changes.

### Surgical edit
Change the smallest span that solves a local issue.

### Rewrite
Rebuild the scene or chapter while preserving specified canon, events, numbers, dialogue commitments, POV, and ending constraints.

### Compare
Produce two meaningfully different solutions and explain the tradeoff. Do not give three cosmetically different paraphrases.

## Acceptance check

Before calling a revision complete:

- structural problem addressed at the correct layer
- no new continuity errors
- no lost plot obligation
- no character knowledge leak
- no accidental change to technical numbers
- voice still recognizable
- newly introduced plants have a planned payoff
- removed scenes did not carry unique setup
- chapter summary and state ledgers updated if canon changed
